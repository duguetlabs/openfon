# OpenFon brand strategy

Version 1 · 27 September 2026 · Brand owner: Cristian

This strategy applies the Brand Building Skills strategy, identity, voice and guidelines workflows to the supplied product brief. The logo is user-approved; the surrounding strategy and applications are the first implemented direction. Business history, demographics, pricing and competitor performance have not been invented to fill a template.

## 01 — What is OpenFon?

**A warm welcome. A clear next step.**

An inquiry often arrives while a business owner is doing the work a customer came for. OpenFon is built around that practical tension: give the caller a useful response and give the owner something clear to follow up. This is a product rationale, not a claimed founder origin story.

OpenFon is a configurable voice receptionist for small businesses. Owners teach it business information, choose a voice, try a browser conversation and review what callers needed. When an answer is unavailable, a useful message is a better outcome than a confident guess. The brand should make that behavior understandable before explaining the underlying AI services.

## 02 — Vision

Make a useful first response accessible to small businesses, with business owners remaining in control of what represents them. A caller should understand what happens next, and an owner should understand what happened.

Welcome · Clarity · Care · Control

## 03 — Mission

Help businesses prepare, test and improve a voice receptionist using their own approved information. Make answers, messages and limitations understandable to both sides of the conversation.

Teach · Try · Review · Improve

## 04 — Values expressed through behavior

| Value | What OpenFon does |
| --- | --- |
| Hospitality | Begin with a useful greeting and a clear invitation to speak. Describe the assistant honestly as AI. |
| Clarity | Show what the caller asked, what was answered, and whether a message was captured. A request is not a completed booking. |
| Practical care | Help the owner take the next step without reading a technical log first. Use calm recovery copy when something fails. |
| Owner control | Keep approved facts, assistant behavior and provider connections understandable and editable. |
| Candor | Explain when information is missing. Make product claims only as strong as the evidence behind them. |

## 05 — Strategic goals

These are proposed directions, not revenue forecasts or commitments to unbuilt features.

1. **Make the purpose obvious.** A new visitor should be able to describe what OpenFon does without knowing what a model provider is.
2. **Earn confidence through trying.** Lead owners toward preparing and testing their receptionist before sharing it.
3. **Make follow-up useful.** Brand the review experience around the caller’s need and message, rather than conversation volume alone.
4. **Stay recognizable across contexts.** Carry the handset-f, blue, butter and direct language from first visit to product, help and shared links.
5. **Serve owners and implementers.** Explain everyday tasks first; provide accurate technical depth where agencies and developers need it.
6. **Learn from real usage.** Later research should examine comprehension, setup-to-test completion and the usefulness of captured messages. No baseline or target is claimed yet.

## 06 — Audience and buying situation

The primary reader is an owner who is responsible for both the work and the customer relationship. They want help handling inquiries while retaining control over what their business says.

| Audience | Situation | Need | Message that matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owner-operator | Working on a repair, serving a customer or running a small studio | A useful first response and a clear message to review | “Keep your hands on your business.” |
| Small-team coordinator | Several people share responsibility for inquiries | Business facts, a consistent greeting and understandable call records | “See what callers needed.” |
| Agency or implementer | Sets up and maintains assistants for clients | Configurable providers, explicit boundaries and reusable assistant recipes | “Set it up around the business.” |

These are task-based audience hypotheses, not interviewed customers. No age, gender or income targeting is justified. English/German support informs copy examples; it does not establish a specific launch country. Workshops and studios are illustrative use cases, not the only intended customers. Sensitive or regulated use cases need their own product validation.

## 07 — Personality

**Welcoming · Capable · Plainspoken · Attentive · Open**

OpenFon behaves like a helpful front-desk colleague who pays attention and leaves a clear note. Its visual warmth makes it easy to approach; its precise language makes it dependable. It can smile in a headline, but it does not make a joke out of a failed call or someone’s concern. The logo supplies personality without turning the whole interface into a cartoon.

Position: conversational rather than formal; quietly upbeat; accessible with technical depth available. Pricing is unknown, so “affordable” and “premium” are not positioning claims.

## 08 — Voice

OpenFon speaks with warm precision: a human rhythm, concrete verbs and honest limits. Start with the customer’s job, then explain the next action. Keep the software’s voice distinct from the customer’s configurable receptionist persona.

The detailed [voice guide](voice.md) specifies vocabulary, tone by situation, English/German examples and the difference between a useful invitation and an unsupported promise.

## 09 — Alternatives and strategic territory

This is a comparison of approaches, not a current vendor benchmark. No named competitor’s quality, pricing or capabilities have been verified in this work.

| Alternative | Why someone chooses it | Question OpenFon needs to answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner answers personally | Direct knowledge and an existing customer relationship | Can the assistant handle the first exchange usefully while the owner is occupied? |
| Voicemail or contact form | Familiar, simple message capture | Does a spoken exchange help clarify what the caller actually needs? |
| Human answering service | Human judgment and coverage arrangements | Which routine inquiries can the business comfortably delegate to software? |
| Configurable voice-agent platform | Technical flexibility and integration options | Can owners do everyday work without navigating infrastructure first? |

Usefulness to the owner and configuration control are the two relevant positioning dimensions. OpenFon’s intended territory is both approachable and configurable. This is a design ambition; it is not proof of superiority over another service.

## 10 — Positioning

**For small businesses that need a useful first response while they are busy, OpenFon is a configurable AI receptionist that answers from approved business information and leaves clear messages for follow-up. Owners can teach it, try it in the browser and review what happened.**

Lead with the useful exchange. Explain “open” through the concrete controls the product supplies: inspectable configuration, choice of compatible providers and portable assistant recipes. Do not turn the name into a promise of universal integrations or arbitrary hosting portability. Open-source licensing can be discussed with the repository’s license; hosted pricing and operational costs are separate.

## 11 — What makes the direction distinctive

**The work stays with the owner. The conversation gets a clear next step.**

The handset-f connects the familiar telephone to the name without needing a second icon. Its rounded Lilita One shapes bring personality; the navy and blue keep it legible and assured. Butter-yellow message slips give the conversation a tangible result: something understandable left for the business.

Across applications, use the same sequence: a question, an answer grounded in a business fact, then a useful message when needed. This is a brand demonstration of the product mechanism. Avoid claims about replacing people, guaranteed availability, response speed or conversion rates unless separately established.

## 12 — Message platform

| Role | Recommended copy | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand line | **A warm welcome. A clear next step.** | Brand book, presentation close, corporate introduction |
| Category descriptor | **An AI receptionist for small businesses.** | Immediately beside aspirational copy when the product is unfamiliar |
| Campaign headline | **Keep your hands on your business.** | Workshop/owner-at-work imagery; adapted from the current welcome story |
| Product explanation | **Teach your receptionist about your business. Try a conversation in your browser. Review what callers needed.** | Homepage, onboarding and product introduction |
| Supporting line | **Your business, in your own words.** | Teaching and configuration content |
| Action | **Try your receptionist** | Only where a real test flow exists; use “Set up your receptionist” if setup is required |

The brand line is recommended in this implementation. It has not been separately approved by the user. Alternative lines explored: “A good place to start” (welcoming but less specific), “A little help with hello” (too playful for routine operations), and “Leave it clear” (concise, but overemphasizes messages). The recommended line balances greeting and outcome.
