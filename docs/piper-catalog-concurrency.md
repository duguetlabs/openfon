# Piper catalog concurrency bounds

[Official report5656369526](https://github.com/duguetlabs/openfon/pull/16#issuecomment-5656369526) identified concurrent misses on reviewed3d0da34507837e861a6e4ebc37b0382f31b6ccfe. The original32-entry LRU applies only after fetch and body parsing, allowing concurrent callers to independently fetch and buffer the same catalog.

## Admission and fallback

One scalar reservation per normalized endpoint is registered synchronously before I/O. At most32 admitted, unsettled catalog lookups exist per isolate. A concurrent miss for an already-pending endpoint, or a new endpoint beyond capacity, immediately returns that caller's static fallback. There is no waiting queue or shared pending promise. Callers share only completed voice maps and choose their own language/fallback. Fresh cache hits remain available at full admission capacity; completed maps retain their independent32-entry LRU and one-hour TTL.

Fetch, response, reader, abort signal and async continuations remain local to the initiating request/Durable Object context. Neither a follower nor admission cleanup awaits work owned by another context. Admission is released only by the initiating lookup's finally after its fetch/read/cancel path settles. Elapsed time alone cannot release another slot or admit a replacement over unresolved old work. The deadline still prevents obsolete cache publication; a response arriving after expiry is cancelled before read/parse begins. Finally checks reservation identity before removal.

**Availability tradeoff:** if an initiating context disappears without running finally, its scalar reservation remains occupied until the isolate is recreated. Enough such events can exhaust dynamic catalog admission in that isolate. Subsequent misses return static voice fallbacks immediately; already-fresh cached maps remain usable. No context destruction or abandoned-slot recovery is claimed. Retaining bounded capacity was chosen over expiry-based reclamation that could overlap unresolved old work.

Manual redirects,1500ms native fetch timeout,65536-byte body limit,64 voice entries, identifier validation, endpoint normalization and refusal to reuse expired/foreign maps remain unchanged. No CallSession, provider routing, audio, receipt or finalizer changes are included. This is a per-isolate concurrent-lookup bound, not a deployment-wide rate limit. Sustained sequential misses may still issue lookups. During cold/expired overlap, callers other than the admitted one may temporarily use a static voice instead of a catalog-specific voice. They never wait for another call's I/O.

## Validation and proof limits

Four original concurrency negatives failed on exact published3d0 under both earlier sharing and admission contracts. The pending-promise candidate passed ordinary tests/native sharing but was replaced because initiating-DO destruction safety was unproven. Final admission tests cover cold/body-pending overlap,32-versus40 distinct misses, TTL/identity/cache behavior, failure/retry and expired unresolved work. Two follow-up controls deliberately ignore AbortSignal:6554efd expiry-reclaim source fails one by awaiting another unresolved lookup (5s test timeout) and one by admitting33 operations. Final source passes27 tests across existing/new catalog files and both worker/web typechecks. These mocked failures prove the changed admission contract, not a native abort defect.

Earlier ordinary actual-workerd request/16-DO/header/body-overlap and nativeHTTP/body failure/retry passes on6554efd are retained. Its old deadline case naturally completed its body at2500ms inside a4s watchdog; fallback/retry alone did not prove native1500ms cancellation. That evidence limitation remains explicit.

The follow-up `test/piper-catalog-deadline-smoke.mjs` uses actual workerd and a loopback HTTP server. First response bodies never end or error naturally. It observes response close-without-finish before harness disposal, measures cancellation timing, verifies retry after the old transport closes, then runs32 distinct-DO lookups with overflow refusal and replacement attempts at expiry. It records whether the old owner had settled and peak physically open response work. Both6554efd and final source passed this discriminating native probe. Final source observed about1540ms caller fallback,1505ms old-body lifetime, no natural finish, physical peak32, and replacement after old transport close; attempts while the old owner was unresolved were observed. The4s watchdog/2200ms reporting tolerance are harness bounds, not increased production timeouts.

Those measurements establish cancellation and ordering in the exercised native cases, not every possible scheduling order or remote socket teardown latency. The source bound is32 admitted unsettled lookup pipelines; it no longer relies on wall-clock expiry implying I/O settlement. No forced initiating-DO destruction is exercised. Client-facing AbortError from earlier probes is not such proof. All Miniflare/server resources are disposed in finally, and no live provider traffic is used.
